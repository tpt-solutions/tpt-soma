tpt-soma: System Design Document (SDD)
Version: 2.0.0
Status: Draft / Architecture Proposal
Domain: Clinical Research Informatics, Bioinformatics, Computational Physiology, Systems Biology
Target Audience: Principal Investigators, Bioinformaticians, Systems Biologists, Translational Researchers, Clinical Trial Designers
1. Executive Summary
tpt-soma is a first-principles, multi-scale computational platform for modeling whole-body human physiology. While modern medicine fragments the body into isolated specialties, human physiology is a deeply coupled, non-linear dynamical system.
tpt-soma bridges the gap between fragmented clinical data, multi-omics, continuous sensor data, and imaging to create mathematically rigorous "Digital Twins" of human physiology. Rather than being siloed by specific pathologies, the platform is architected around the fundamental biological scales and methodologies of modern research. Crucially, it provides a unified framework to study the translational manifestations of the modern chronic disease triad—metabolic dysfunction (diabetes), cellular dysregulation (cancer), and systemic decline (longevity/aging)—by grounding disease research in the fundamental, healthy baseline data of the entire body.
2. Core Philosophy & First Principles
The Body is a Network, Not a Collection of Parts: We do not model organs in isolation. We model the biochemical, neural, and vascular edges between them. Systemic inflammation directly alters the tumor microenvironment and accelerates epigenetic drift.
Mathematical Correctness: Biological simulations often fail due to floating-point accumulation errors. tpt-soma utilizes exact rational numbers and fixed-point arithmetic for critical metabolic flux calculations, ensuring deterministic and reproducible results.
Proof-Native Security: Built on the principles of tpt-archon, the system uses capability-based security. A researcher cannot query a patient's genomic data unless they hold a cryptographically verified capability token for that specific cohort and data class.
Compute at the Edge of Data: We do not move petabytes of raw sequencing data to the compute layer. We push lightweight, WebAssembly (Wasm) sandboxed compute to the data layer.
3. The "Whole Body" Conceptual Model: The Ontological Soma Graph (OSG)
To model the whole body, tpt-soma abandons traditional relational schemas for its core state representation. Instead, it uses the Ontological Soma Graph (OSG).
Nodes (Entities):
Macro: Organ Systems, Organs, Tissues.
Micro: Cell types, specific cell instances (in spatial transcriptomics).
Molecular: Proteins, metabolites, genes, epigenetic markers.
Edges (Relationships & Flux):
Physical: Vascular connections, neural pathways, physical tissue boundaries.
Biochemical: Ligand-receptor bindings, metabolic pathway reactions, signaling cascades.
State Vectors: Every node possesses a time-series state vector. For a "Liver Hepatocyte" node, the state vector includes its current transcriptomic profile, intracellular metabolite concentrations, and senescence markers.
The Systemic Cross-Talk Engine: The OSG allows researchers to run queries like: "Simulate the effect of a 20% reduction in adipose tissue insulin sensitivity on the systemic circulation of IGF-1, and project the resulting impact on proliferation rates in a simulated breast tissue node over 5 years."
4. System Architecture
The architecture is divided into four distinct layers, prioritizing high-performance data ingestion and secure, isolated compute.
Layer 1: The Ingestion & Normalization Edge (Rust / no_std)
Function: Ingests raw, messy, real-world data and normalizes it into the OSG.
Components:
tpt-soma-ingest: High-throughput, zero-allocation (no_std) parsers for FHIR R5 (clinical), FASTQ/BAM (genomics), and raw binary streams from CGMs (Dexcom/Libre) and wearables.
tpt-soma-harmonize: Maps disparate local ontologies to standard ontologies (LOINC, SNOMED, HPO, UBERON) using deterministic mapping tables and LLM-assisted fuzzy matching.
Layer 2: The Soma Core (Storage & State)
A polyglot persistence layer optimized for the specific shapes of biological data.
The Graph Store (Topology): A high-performance graph database to store the OSG topology and cross-system relationships.
The Time-Series Store (Trajectories): DuckDB or ClickHouse for high-speed, columnar storage of longitudinal biomarker trajectories and continuous wearable data.
The Object Store (Blobs): S3-compatible storage for raw multi-omics files, whole-slide images, and 3D medical imaging.
Layer 3: The Simulation & Analytics Engine
Differential Equation Solvers: Rust-based ODE/PDE solvers for modeling pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics (PK/PD) and metabolic flux.
Multi-Omics & Pipeline Integration: Containerized execution of standard bioinformatics pipelines (Nextflow) ensuring absolute reproducibility.
Digital Twin Engine: Generates patient-specific computational models by calibrating the baseline OSG against the patient's actual multi-omics and clinical baseline.
Layer 4: The Researcher Interface
Web Frontend: React/TypeScript with WebGL for 3D rendering of the Soma Graph and spatial anatomy.
Notebook Integration: Deep integration with JupyterLab and RStudio via high-performance Arrow/Flight RPC interfaces.
5. Domain Modules (The Biological Scales)
Rather than being siloed by disease, tpt-soma is organized by the biological scale and methodology of human research.
A. tpt-soma-genomica (Molecular & Multi-Omics)
The molecular scale: The fundamental code and chemistry of the body.
Genomics & Epigenomics: Variant calling, GWAS, DNA methylation analysis, chromatin accessibility.
Transcriptomics & Proteomics: Bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, mass spectrometry proteomics, protein-protein interaction networks.
Metabolomics & Lipidomics: Small molecule profiling, lipid panel analysis, metabolic pathway flux.
Microbiomics: 16S rRNA and shotgun metagenomics, host-microbiome interaction modeling.
Multi-Omics Integration: Tools to correlate and integrate data across all molecular layers.
B. tpt-soma-cytos (Cellular & Tissue Biology)
The cellular and tissue scale: Understanding the body at the level of cells and tissue architecture.
Single-Cell/Nucleus Analysis: Pipelines for scRNA-seq and snRNA-seq to classify cell types and states across all tissues.
Spatial Biology: Visualization and analysis of spatial transcriptomics and proteomics mapped to tissue architecture.
Digital Pathology: Whole-slide image (WSI) analysis, computational histopathology, tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) quantification.
Cell-Cell Communication: Modeling ligand-receptor interactions and signaling networks within and between tissues.
C. tpt-soma-organon (Organ Systems & Physiology)
The organ scale: Modeling the function and dysfunction of individual organ systems.
Organ-Specific Modeling: Dedicated modules for cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, hepatic, endocrine, and immune systems.
Organ Imaging: Ingestion and analysis of MRI, CT, ultrasound, and PET data specific to each organ system.
Organ Function Tests: Tools for analyzing ejection fraction, GFR, pulmonary function tests, liver enzyme panels, etc.
Cross-Organ Coupling: Modeling how dysfunction in one organ cascades to affect others.
D. tpt-soma-chronos (Longitudinal & Temporal Dynamics)
The time scale: Tracking how the body changes over time, from milliseconds to decades.
Wearable & Sensor Data: Ingestion of continuous data from CGMs, ECG monitors, blood pressure cuffs, activity trackers.
Longitudinal Biomarker Trajectories: Tracking standard blood panels and clinical scores over time. Calculating rates of change, not just snapshots.
Circadian & Ultradian Rhythms: Analyzing how biological processes oscillate over 24-hour and shorter cycles.
Disease Progression Modeling: Statistical and ML models for predicting how diseases evolve over time.
E. tpt-soma-simulacrum (Computational Physiology & Digital Twins)
The modeling scale: Creating mathematically rigorous in-silico models of human physiology.
Systems Biology & Pathway Modeling: ODE models of metabolic pathways, signaling cascades, and gene regulatory networks.
Pharmacokinetics/Pharmacodynamics (PK/PD): Modeling how drugs are absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and excreted.
Whole-Body Digital Twins: Integrating all scales into patient-specific computational models that simulate whole-body physiology.
Differentiable Physiology: Integration with PyTorch/JAX to allow researchers to fit computational models to empirical data using gradient descent.
F. tpt-soma-pathos (Translational Pathology)
The disease scale: This is where your original three pillars live, alongside all other disease domains.
Metabolic & Endocrine (Diabetes): Insulin resistance modeling, CGM trajectory analysis, metabolic syndrome.
Oncology (Cancer): Solid tumors, hematologic malignancies, tumor microenvironment, immunotherapy response.
Longevity & Aging: Biological aging clocks, senescence tracking, age-related diseases, geriatrics.
Cardiovascular, Autoimmune, Infectious: Dedicated sub-modules for heart failure, rheumatological diseases, and pathogen genomics.
G. tpt-soma-clinica (Clinical Research & Trials)
The translational/clinical scale: Bridging the gap between bench research and patient care.
EHR & FHIR Integration: Ingestion of electronic health records using FHIR R5 standards.
Clinical Trial Design & Management: Cohort discovery, patient recruitment, trial protocol design, adverse event tracking.
Biomarker Discovery & Validation: Statistical pipelines for identifying and validating diagnostic, prognostic, and predictive biomarkers.
Real-World Evidence (RWE): Analytics on large observational datasets to generate evidence outside of traditional clinical trials.
6. Security, Privacy, and Compliance
Medical research data requires uncompromising security. tpt-soma implements a Zero-Trust, Proof-Native security model.
Capability-Based Access Control (CBAC): Access is granted via unforgeable cryptographic capabilities. If a researcher lacks the capability for a specific data class, the database physically cannot execute the query.
Differential Privacy for Cohorts: When exporting aggregate statistics, tpt-soma automatically injects calibrated mathematical noise to ensure no individual patient's data can be reverse-engineered.
Immutable Audit Trails: Every data access and pipeline execution is logged in an append-only, cryptographically hashed ledger for HIPAA/GDPR compliance.
Data Residency & Sovereignty: Supports "federated compute" for institutions that cannot let data leave their borders.
7. Technology Stack
Core Systems & Parsers: Rust (no_std for edge parsers, std for backend). Guarantees memory safety and zero-cost abstractions.
Data Formats: Apache Arrow / Parquet for analytical data exchange; Protocol Buffers for high-speed serialization.
Databases: DuckDB (embedded analytics), Graph DB (topology), S3 (object/blob).
Compute & Sandboxing: WebAssembly (Wasm) for executing untrusted researcher code safely.
Orchestration: Kubernetes for cloud deployment; Nextflow for bioinformatics pipeline orchestration.
Frontend: React, TypeScript, WebGL for 3D anatomical rendering.
8. Phased Delivery Roadmap
Building a whole-body simulator from day one is impossible. We will use a phased approach, expanding from foundational molecular data to systemic simulation, and finally to translational applications.
Phase 1: The Molecular & Cellular Wedge (Months 1-6)
Focus: tpt-soma-genomica and tpt-soma-cytos.
Deliverable: High-performance ingestion and visualization for multi-omics, single-cell transcriptomics, and spatial biology. Prove the no_std edge-ingestion for massive genomic datasets.
Goal: Win over bioinformaticians and molecular biologists by solving their data-wrangling and multi-omics integration pain points.
Phase 2: The Physiological & Temporal Expansion (Months 7-12)
Focus: tpt-soma-organon and tpt-soma-chronos.
Deliverable: Ingestion of organ-specific imaging, EHR data (FHIR), and continuous wearable/CGM data. Build the longitudinal trajectory engines.
Goal: Transition from looking at "cells and genes" to looking at "organs and time." Bridge the gap between molecular biology and macroscopic physiology.
Phase 3: The Computational & Digital Twin Core (Months 13-18)
Focus: tpt-soma-simulacrum.
Deliverable: Deploy the ODE/PDE solvers for metabolic flux and PK/PD. Integrate differentiable physiology frameworks.
Goal: Move from observing the body to simulating it. Enable researchers to create and calibrate patient-specific Digital Twins.
Phase 4: Translational Pathology & Clinical Integration (Months 19-24+)
Focus: tpt-soma-pathos and tpt-soma-clinica.
Deliverable: The disease modules (focusing heavily on the Longevity, Cancer, Diabetes triad) and the clinical trial management tools. Connect with tpt-cerebrum to ingest neurological data for whole-body modeling.
Goal: Achieve the ultimate vision of predictive, mechanistic translational medicine, directly impacting clinical trial design and patient outcomes.
9. Conclusion & The Unified tpt Ecosystem
By structuring tpt-soma around the fundamental biological scales and methods of research, it becomes a foundational platform for the entire field of human physiology, rather than a niche disease tracker.
When combined with tpt-cerebrum, you create a unified, first-principles computational ecosystem for whole-human physiology. A researcher studying the metabolic origins of Alzheimer's ("Type 3 Diabetes") can use tpt-soma to model systemic insulin resistance, CGM data, and multi-omics, and seamlessly query tpt-cerebrum to see how that systemic dysfunction is altering cerebral glucose metabolism and accelerating brain aging.
